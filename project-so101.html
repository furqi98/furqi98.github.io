SO-101: Learning Manipulation from Demonstration

A full learning-from-demonstration stack on a physical SO-101 arm. Demonstrations are collected by teleoperation, recorded as synchronised dual-camera video and joint trajectories, and used to train vision-language-action policies for pick-and-place. Datasets and trained policies are public on the Hugging Face Hub.

Result

The ACT policy trained on pick_place_1_grid completed 7 of 8 pick-and-place trials. The block was repositioned by hand between attempts rather than reset to a fixed pose, so the policy had to generalise across starting positions rather than replay a single trajectory.

Engineering

Cross-environment teleoperation

The LeRobot teleoperator stack and Isaac Sim require incompatible Python environments and cannot share a process. The leader arm is therefore read in its own interpreter and streamed as six packed joint radians over a local UDP socket at roughly 50 Hz, with per-joint sign and offset correction adjustable from JSON while the simulator keeps running — so calibration is a live loop rather than a restart cycle.

Iteration speed as a design goal

A persistent pick-place loop launches Isaac Sim once and re-reads its tuning parameters every cycle. Grasp depth, object placement and gripper travel are tuned without a relaunch, turning a multi-minute iteration into seconds. Most of the useful parameter search happened only because it became cheap enough to actually do.

LeRobot Studio

A local web app I wrote so teleoperation, recording, training and inference launch without hand-composing CLI invocations. It builds and supervises the commands and reads datasets and checkpoints from disk — it never modifies the LeRobot source tree.

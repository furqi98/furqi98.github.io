Teaching a Robot to Pick and Place Through Demonstration

A study of how demonstration coverage affects imitation-learning generalization.

I trained an ACT policy on a physical SO-101 arm to pick up a cube and place it in a box, changing only how the demonstrations were collected. With the cube placed randomly across the workspace, the policy struggled to reach or grasp it. I then divided the workspace into a six-cell grid and recorded placements in each cell: the policy learned to localize the cube but could not grasp it reliably. Finally, I restricted the demonstrations to a single cell with 19 marked positions and 4 cube orientations at each. The policy trained on this structured dataset enabled the SO-101 arm to successfully reach the cube, orient the gripper, grasp the cube and place it in the box.

  1. Research question

    Does more demonstration data necessarily produce better imitation learning, or does where the data comes from matter more?

  3. Experimental design

    Same robot, task and policy. Only the spatial and orientation coverage of the demonstrations changed, across three datasets.

  5. Result

    76 structured demonstrations (30K frames) outperformed 101 demonstrations (55K frames) within the demonstrated region.

The experiment

Three datasets, one variable

I kept the task (pick a cube, place it in a box), the physical SO-101 leader–follower arm and the ACT policy fixed, and changed only the structure of the demonstrations.

  1. 01UnstructuredCube placed freely across the workspace.101 demos → unreliable localization and grasping
  3. 02Six-gridCube distributed across six spatial regions.101 demos · 55K frames → coarse localization, unreliable grasping
  5. 03Structured position + orientation19 locations × 4 orientations = 76 configurations.76 demos · 30K frames → reliable pick-and-place within the demonstrated region

The result

Coverage, not dataset size

02 · Six-grid

101 demonstrations

55,001 frames

Coarse localization, unreliable grasping.

03 · Structured

76 demonstrations

30,110 frames

Reliable pick-and-place within the demonstrated region.

Finding: dataset size alone did not explain performance. The structured 76-episode dataset produced more reliable behavior within its demonstrated region than the 101-episode six-grid dataset, while spatial regions absent from the demonstrations remained difficult for the policy.

More data ≠ more coverage.

Why this matters

Next: generalization in VLA policies

The failure outside the demonstrated region motivates my interest in robot foundation models: can pretrained Vision-Language-Action policies generalize beyond the states represented in physical demonstrations?

I have fine-tuned SmolVLA (0.5B) on the six-grid dataset and am evaluating its physical generalization.

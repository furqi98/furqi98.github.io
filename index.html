Hi, I’m Syed Furqan Ali. I am interested in Embodied AI, particularly in how robots and drones can become more autonomous by giving them the ability to perceive, reason about, and act in the physical world.

My research exploration focuses on using vision-language and language models as the intelligence layer of embodied systems, connecting visual perception and natural-language instructions to physical actions.

I have explored this through several robotic platforms. In Survey-RAG, I investigated whether a drone could understand natural-language instructions and use a visual memory of its environment to navigate to a desired target. In Hexagrid, I developed a closed-loop system where a VLM evaluates its visual surroundings and autonomously selects a safe landing location. More recently, I have been working with the SO-101 robotic arm, collecting multiple demonstration datasets and training imitation-learning policies, including an ACT policy that successfully performs pick-and-place tasks.

Through these projects, I am interested in understanding how foundation models can move beyond perception and language to become reliable decision-making and action mechanisms for physical robots.

I am currently applying for PhD positions in embodied AI and robot learning.

Embodied AI projects

Research interests

  • Vision-language-action policies: what actually transfers between architectures, pretraining and data
  • Open-vocabulary spatial memory: letting a robot answer questions about a place when the question was unknown at capture time
  • Foundation models under embodied constraints: which competence belongs at which latency and energy tier
  • Learning from demonstration: what makes a demonstration dataset good rather than merely large
  • Evaluation methodology: benchmarks that can actually be wrong, and ground-truth hygiene
  • Aerial autonomy for search and rescue: one flight, no connectivity, unknown queries

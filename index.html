Syed Furqan Ali

I build robots that take instructions in plain language. My work sits at the point where vision-language models meet systems that have to act under real constraints — limited onboard compute, a finite battery, and no assumption of connectivity.

Most recently I built Survey-RAG, a drone that surveys an area once and afterwards answers requests like "go to the park with the fountain" by retrieving the matching aerial view and flying to it; and an imitation-learning stack on the SO-101 manipulator that learns pick-and-place from teleoperated demonstration. I care as much about measuring these systems honestly as about building them.

I am applying for PhD positions in embodied AI and robot learning.

Research interests

I want to work on agents that build their own usable memory of the physical world and act on language instructions in it. Concretely:

Vision-language-action policies

End-to-end policies that map pixels and instructions to actions. I have trained and compared ACT, SmolVLA, π0.5 and a diffusion-transformer flow-matching policy on the same task, and I am interested in what actually transfers between them — architecture, pretraining, or data.

Open-vocabulary spatial memory

Representations that let a robot answer questions about a place it has already visited, when the question was not known at capture time. The retrieval index, not the caption, is the interesting object: captions are lossy compression committed to before the query exists.

Foundation models under embodied constraints

A VLM that takes seconds per call cannot sit in a control loop. I am interested in the architecture question this forces: which competence belongs at which latency and energy tier, and what a system gives up when the large model is not always available.

Learning from demonstration

Teleoperation, data collection, and what makes a demonstration dataset good rather than merely large — especially when a real leader arm drives a simulated follower and the sim-to-real gap runs in both directions.

Evaluation methodology

Benchmarks that can actually be wrong. My own first evaluation was circular and inverted the method ranking; catching it changed which conclusion I believed. I think ground-truth hygiene is undervalued relative to how often it decides published results.

Aerial autonomy & search-and-rescue

Post-disaster response as a forcing function: one flight, no connectivity, unknown queries, and a real cost to getting it wrong. It makes the resource constraints concrete instead of hypothetical.

Current work

Ongoing threads, mostly aimed at the weakest parts of my own results rather than the strongest.

Energy accounting on real flight hardware

The battery-tiered design was an argument until I measured it. On the drone's actual companion computer — a Jetson Orin NX 16GB, sampled through tegrastats board input power — indexing a full 192-snap survey on GPU costs ~270 J, about 0.10% of a 74 Wh battery, and a fully loaded query costs ~7 J. Against propulsion, compute is 4–6% of total draw. The interesting number is the asymmetry: a SigLIP2 image embed is 0.14 J, a VLM verification is 4.37 J — roughly 30× — which is precisely why the VLM belongs at a conditional tier rather than in the retrieval path.

Multi-environment generalization

Every number in my report comes from a single 800×800 m park survey, which is the honest reviewer-facing weakness of the work. I am flying the unchanged pipeline across the other packaged simulation environments, without touching the original code path, so each new map is simultaneously a generalization datapoint and a contamination-free training corpus — the eval set cannot double as training data without the result becoming circular.

Domain-pretrained retrieval backends

Testing whether aerial-specific pretraining beats general vision-language pretraining on this task: SkyCLIP against SigLIP2, and a LoRA fine-tune on remote-sensing captions, all scored on the same human-labelled benchmark with the same ten-vector indexing. So far the general backend holds up better than expected, which is itself the finding.

Judge robustness

If a VLM grades my results, the conclusions should survive changing the grader. I am re-running judged evaluations under a different model family to separate what the method does from what one particular judge believes about it.

Projects

Survey-RAG: Language-Queryable Aerial Memory for One-Pass Surveys

Solo project · AirSim + PX4 SITL · CPU-only, fully local

A drone surveys an area once, converts everything it sees into a searchable spatial memory, and afterwards answers natural-language requests by retrieving the matching view in milliseconds and flying to its recorded coordinates. Each snap is indexed as five tiles under two renderings rather than one global embedding, which recovers most of what an open-vocabulary detector would add at zero extra model cost. Compute is placed in four battery tiers, so the VLM never sits in the retrieval path — it supplies vocabulary offline and verification only when the top-1/top-2 margin is thin.

  • 0.93 P@1 [0.84–1.00] on 44 human-labelled queries — against 0.86 for embeddings alone, 0.68 for an OWLv2 detector baseline, and 0.64 for a caption/BM25 baseline.
  • 7/8 typed requests flown correctly in one closed-loop flight, median arrival error 19.1 m — inside the 60×45 m camera footprint.
  • Generalises with zero retuning to three real-world datasets; on Hurricane Harvey UAV imagery a single query enriches genuinely flooded scenes in the top-20 by 6× over chance.
  • A measured floor, not a vague one. Objects below ~1% of frame area defeat every configuration tested — and the single closed-loop failure was exactly the object the benchmark predicted would fail.

Imitation Learning on the SO-101 Manipulator

Solo project · LeRobot + Isaac Sim · physical leader arm

A full learning-from-demonstration stack. A physical SO-101 leader arm drives a simulated follower in Isaac Sim over a 50 Hz UDP bridge — the two need incompatible Python environments, so the leader is read in its own process and streamed as packed joint radians, with per-joint sign and offset correction tunable live while the simulator keeps running. Demonstrations are recorded as synchronised state, action, top-camera and wrist-camera streams, and the resulting dataset trains vision-language-action policies.

  • 7/8 successful pick-and-place trials under the trained ACT policy, with the block repositioned by hand between attempts rather than reset to a fixed pose.
  • Four policy families trained and compared on the same task — ACT, SmolVLA, π0.5, and DiT-Flow, a diffusion transformer with a flow-matching objective integrated as a LeRobot policy package.
  • Iteration speed as a design goal. A persistent pick-place loop launches Isaac Sim once and re-reads its tuning parameters every cycle, so grasp depth, object placement and gripper travel are tuned without a relaunch.
  • LeRobot Studio — a local web app I wrote so teleoperation, recording, training and inference launch without hand-composing CLI invocations. It builds and supervises the commands; it never modifies the LeRobot source tree.

Hexagrid: VLM-Guided Precision Landing

Solo project · AirSim + PX4 · Qwen2.5-VL

Autonomous landing-site selection with a vision-language model in the decision loop. Six candidate views are composited into a single labelled mosaic so the VLM compares them in one forward pass — comparison is the actual task, and framing it that way costs one inference instead of six. A numbered grid is then overlaid on the winning view and repeatedly re-queried, with cell scores decayed and boosted until one crosses a lock threshold, so no single hallucinated answer can commit the aircraft to a landing.

  • Confidence lock rather than a single verdict — the decay/boost loop turns an unreliable one-shot judgement into a stable decision.
  • Reasoning surfaced to the operator. Every phase pushes its overlay and status line into the QGroundControl live video feed, so the operator watches which view was chosen and which cell is winning, not a raw camera stream.
  • Composes with Survey-RAG — hands off from the arrival hold, making search and landing a single pipeline.

Selected results

From the Survey-RAG benchmark: 56 stratified queries spanning object size classes, phrasing variants, compositional queries and negative queries for absent objects; ground truth from hand-labelling 192 images across 14 categories; 95% bootstrap confidence intervals.

A note on ground truth

My first evaluation graded retrieval using pseudo-ground-truth derived from the system's own captions. Under that scheme a keyword baseline "won" at 0.85 — because it was searching the answer key. Hand-labelling all 192 images inverted the ranking: that baseline was in fact the worst method at 0.64. Ground-truth hygiene decided which conclusion was real, and I would not have caught it without doing the labelling myself.

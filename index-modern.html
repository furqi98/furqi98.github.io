01 Research interest

I work on grounding language models in physical systems that have to act under real constraints — limited onboard compute, limited battery, and no assumption of connectivity.

The thread running through my projects is that foundation models are most useful when they are placed where their cost is affordable, not simply dropped into the control loop. In my aerial work a vision-language model never touches the millisecond retrieval path; it supplies vocabulary offline, and honesty — the ability to say “not here” — only when retrieval is genuinely ambiguous. That framing came out of measurement, not intuition, and measuring it honestly meant discovering that my first evaluation was circular and throwing the result away.

I am looking for a PhD position where I can push this further: open-vocabulary spatial memory, vision-language-action policies, and the evaluation methodology that keeps claims about them trustworthy.

02 Selected projects

Aerial autonomy · Open-vocabulary retrieval

Survey-RAG — Language-Queryable Aerial Memory

A drone surveys an area once, converts everything it sees into a searchable spatial memory, and afterwards answers natural-language requests — “go to the park with the fountain” — by retrieving the matching view in milliseconds and flying to its recorded coordinates. Built for post-disaster response, where the question is never known at capture time, flights are the scarce resource, and connectivity cannot be assumed.

What makes it work

  • Battery-tiered compute. Four tiers place each model where its energy cost is affordable — image embedding in flight, cosine retrieval at query time, a local VLM only when the top-1/top-2 margin is thin, and captioning offline while charging. A no-VLM configuration costs only −0.07 P@1.
  • Tiled dual-render embeddings instead of a detector. Each snap is indexed as 5 tiles × 2 renderings (raw and deglared), max-pooled at query time. A fountain is tiny in a 60×45 m footprint and a single global embedding dilutes it; quadrant embeddings recover most of what an open-vocabulary detector would add, with zero extra models — and the winning quadrant gives the flight controller a quarter-footprint offset toward the object.
  • Geometric non-overlap. Snap centres are planned one camera footprint apart and flown serpentine with yaw locked to zero, so image-up is North in every frame and no two snaps overlap.

What I measured

  • P@1 0.93 [0.84–1.00] on 44 human-labelled queries, against 0.86 for embeddings alone and 0.64 for a caption/BM25 baseline.
  • Beat an open-vocabulary detector baseline — OWLv2 reached 0.68 P@1 and had the worst false-confidence on absent objects of any method tested.
  • Generalised with zero retuning to three real-world datasets — including 1.00 judged P@1 on real geotagged Ohio imagery and, on Hurricane Harvey UAV footage, 6× enrichment of genuinely flooded scenes in the top-20.
  • A scoped, honest failure. Objects below ~1% of frame area defeat every configuration tested — and the one closed-loop flight that missed was exactly the object the benchmark predicted would miss.

The lesson I keep

My first evaluation graded retrieval using pseudo-ground-truth derived from the system's own captions. Under that scheme a keyword baseline “won” at 0.85 — because it was searching the answer key. Hand-labelling all 192 images across 14 categories inverted the ranking: that baseline was in fact the worst method at 0.64. Ground-truth hygiene decided which conclusion was real, and I would not have caught it without doing the labelling myself.

Manipulation · Vision-language-action policies

SO-101 — Learning Manipulation from Demonstration

A full imitation-learning stack on the SO-101 arm: a physical leader arm drives a simulated follower in Isaac Sim over a 50 Hz UDP bridge, demonstrations are recorded with synchronised top and wrist cameras, and the resulting dataset trains vision-language-action policies for pick-and-place.

The pipeline I built

  • Cross-environment teleoperation. The real SO-101 leader arm and Isaac Sim need incompatible Python environments, so the leader is read in its own process and streamed as packed joint radians over a local UDP socket — with per-joint sign and offset correction tunable live from JSON while the simulator keeps running.
  • Scripted and teleoperated data collection. A persistent pick-place loop launches Isaac Sim once and re-reads its tuning parameters every cycle, so grasp depth, object position and gripper travel can be tuned without a relaunch — turning a multi-minute iteration into seconds.
  • Dataset. Episodes recorded as synchronised state, action, top-camera and wrist-camera streams, in LeRobot format.

Policies trained & compared

  • ACT — action-chunking transformer, the imitation-learning baseline. 7/8 successful pick-and-place trials with the block repositioned by hand between every attempt: the policy sees only camera images and joint states, and is never told where the block is.
  • SmolVLA — a compact vision-language-action model, trained locally.
  • π0.5 — flow-matching VLA, fine-tuned on the recorded task.
  • DiT-Flow — a diffusion-transformer policy with a flow-matching objective, integrated as a LeRobot policy package.

Tooling

  • LeRobot Studio — a local web app I wrote so teleoperation, recording, training and inference can be launched without composing CLI invocations by hand. It never modifies the LeRobot source tree; it builds and supervises the commands.

Aerial autonomy · VLM-in-the-loop control

Hexagrid — VLM-Guided Precision Landing

Autonomous landing-site selection with a vision-language model in the decision loop. The drone captures six candidate views, a single VLM call chooses the safest, and a confidence loop then locks onto one cell of a grid overlaid on the winner before descent.

Design

  • One call, not six. The six candidate snaps are composited into a single labelled 3×2 mosaic so the VLM compares them in one forward pass instead of scoring each independently — comparison is the actual task, and it costs one inference.
  • Confidence lock, not a single verdict. A numbered 4×4 grid is overlaid on the winning view and repeatedly re-queried, with cell scores decayed and boosted until one crosses a lock threshold — so a single hallucinated answer cannot commit the aircraft to a landing.
  • World-frame alignment converts the locked cell into a metric offset for the flight controller.

Operator interface

  • Every phase pushes its overlay and a status line into the QGroundControl live video feed — so the operator watches the reasoning (which view was chosen, which cell is winning) rather than a raw camera stream.
  • Hands off from the Survey-RAG arrival hold, making the two systems a single search-and-land pipeline.
